On August 2nd, I did something that my typical routine doesn't include: I spent the entire night at the beach. (★´∀｀★)

Usually, my summer nights are spent in my room with the AC running, listening to the hum of my PC and browsing retro webrings. But this time, I traded the glow of my monitors for the real-world dark of the Adriatic coast. 

It was a beautiful night, a sensory experience that felt like stepping out of the virtual world and directly into a high-resolution, uncompressed reality.

## Under a Tiled Sky of Stars (◡‿◡✿)

When you live in a city, you forget what the sky actually looks like. The ambient glow of streetlights and apartments acts like a low-opacity layer over the stars, flattening them into a gray void. 

But out there, away from the city center, the light pollution faded. 

Looking up was like staring at an ancient, perfect space wallpaper—except it wasn't a tiled 88x31 background GIF on a GeoCities homepage. It was infinitely deep, dark, and clear. Thousands of tiny, pinprick pixels of light scattered across the black canvas, completely static yet alive. 

We spent hours just walking along the shoreline, the wet sand cool beneath our feet, eating simple food, and listening to the rhythmic, low-frequency sound of the waves. It felt like the physical equivalent of a ambient vaporwave track playing in a loop.

## The Midnight Dip and the Biting Chill (￣﹏￣；)

Around midnight, we decided to go for a swim. 

Stepping into the sea in complete darkness is terrifying at first. The water looks pitch black, like a bottomless pool of ink. But once you dive in, the fear dissolves. The water felt thick, cool, and incredibly calm. Floating on my back, looking at the stars while suspended in the sea, was a moment of absolute silence. My mind, usually cluttered with code syntax, UI layouts, and social worries, felt completely clear.

But the real challenge started when we stepped out of the water. 

The night wind had picked up, and without the sun to warm the air, the cold was sharp and biting. Wrapping myself in a towel, shivering on the beach chair, I felt a deep, refreshing chill that you can never replicate indoors. It was cold, yes, but it made me feel incredibly alert and alive.

## The Morning Oven (￣﹏￣；)

And then, the sun rose. 

While the night was a beautiful, cool dream, the morning was a harsh reality check. As soon as the sun peeked over the horizon, the temperature didn't just rise—it spiked. 

Within an hour, the crisp sea air was replaced by a heavy, sticky, suffocating heat. The humidity turned the beach into an oven, and the sand became too hot to walk on. That early morning heat is honestly the worst part of summer; it makes you feel instantly tired, sweaty, and wishing for the cool cover of the night to return. It really sucks (fa davvero schifo).

Still, despite the morning heatwave, I wouldn't trade that night for anything. It proved to me, once again, that the best experiences happen when you shut down the screens and let the real world overwrite your routines.
